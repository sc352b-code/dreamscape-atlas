# Dreamscape Tarot content-quality standard

This is the editorial/content QA layer for Tarot Engine v1. It sits alongside the structural engine contract.

A Tarot is not good enough merely because it validates structurally. It must reveal something meaningful about the user's corpus in language a non-specialist can understand.

## Reader test

After reading the full Tarot, the user should be able to answer:

- How important is this subject in my corpus?
- Where does it tend to appear?
- What repeatedly happens around it?
- What seems to change its meaning from dream to dream?
- What other things genuinely recur with it, if that has been verified?
- Does its role change over time, or is there no clear chronology?
- Which actual dreams support the reading?
- What possible meanings are suggested by the corpus?
- Which claims are evidence and which are interpretation?

If the Tarot cannot answer these, it is not yet content-complete.

## Chapter purposes

### Overview

Must communicate:
- prevalence in the whole corpus;
- appearances versus unique dreams where relevant;
- one concise corpus-level synthesis;
- the variable(s) that most change the subject's significance.

Avoid:
- generic symbolism;
- repeating the same sentences used in Recurring patterns.

### Where it appears

Must communicate:
- distribution across territories/places supported by evidence;
- count semantics;
- percentages or other reader-friendly scale where useful;
- overlap logic when memberships are non-exclusive.

A bar chart alone is not enough. The reader should understand what the geography says and what it does not say.

### Recurring patterns

Must communicate:
- repeated functions/actions, not abstract symbolic definitions;
- evidence strength;
- plain-English definition of confidence labels;
- enough detail to distinguish the patterns from one another.

If detailed pattern cards would overflow, paginate individual patterns rather than shorten them into vague labels.

### Appears alongside

This chapter is only a true co-occurrence chapter when same-dream recurrence has been verified.

If co-occurrence is not yet verified:
- say so explicitly;
- do not imply frequency;
- related Atlas elements may be shown only as a navigation constellation;
- mark the chapter analytically provisional.

Do not turn map proximity or thematic similarity into an “appears with” claim.

### How it changes

Only describe chronology when the source analysis demonstrates chronology.

If it does not:
- say that no strong progression has been established;
- identify recurring modes if useful;
- do not manufacture a developmental story.

An honest “no clear chronology” is better evidence than an invented arc.

### Source dreams

Must provide a doorway to the private source corpus.

The Tarot itself should not contain dozens of records or raw private dream text.

The user should be able to reach the source dreams from the Tarot when the private provider is connected.

### Possible meanings

Must begin from the corpus pattern, not from a symbol dictionary.

A strong interpretation section includes:
- corpus-rooted possible readings;
- the role of the dreamer's actions/agency;
- literal/biographical possibilities;
- theoretical lenses where useful;
- a clear interpretive boundary.

Avoid:
- “X means Y” formulations;
- theory that could have been written without seeing the corpus.

## Reader-friendly writing rules

- Prefer concrete verbs: cross, enter, protect, contain, flee, play, care.
- Explain specialist terms immediately.
- One principal insight per companion page.
- Use the user's corpus as the subject of the sentence.
- Keep evidence and interpretation visually and verbally distinct.
- Do not repeat the same claim across several tabs merely to fill space.
- A sentence should earn its place by adding evidence, distinction or meaning.

## Content completeness rule for future Tarots

A chapter may be:
- **supported**;
- **provisional / analysis incomplete**;
- **insufficient evidence**;
- **private-source dependent**.

Do not disguise the latter three as “supported” by filling the card with generic language.

The goal is not seven equally full tabs. The goal is seven honest windows onto what the corpus actually supports.
